Memory Pairs — lock facts so they show up in the hall
-----------------------------------------------------

What you do:
    Pick a subject and topic. Match term ↔ answer. Beat your best time. Earn stars.

Why this helps UPSC Prelims:

    Prelims rewards retrieval under pressure, not “I’ve read this somewhere.”
    Schemes, Articles, dynasties, capitals, reports, geography pairs —
    UPSC often asks the exact pairing:
        which ministry / which year / which Article / which site.

    • Active recall beats rereading notes.
      Matching forces you to pull the answer from memory —
      the same muscle you need when options look familiar but only one is right.

    • Speed matters.
      Faster pairing = less panic in a 2-hour paper when a factual Q lands mid-set.

    • Topic sprints fit real life.
      5–10 minutes between lectures, before sleep, or as a warm-up before a mock.

    [SCREENSHOT: Memory Pairs mid-game]

Timeline Sorter — stop losing marks to chronology traps
-------------------------------------------------------

What you do:
    Drag shuffled events into the right order. Hit Check. See years + a short explanation.

Why this helps UPSC Prelims:

    History and polity questions often don’t ask “what year was X?” —
    they ask which came first, arrange in chronological order,
    or hide order inside multi-statement options.

    Acts of the British period, freedom-struggle events, commissions,
    constitutional amendments — one wrong sequence and the whole answer falls.

    • Sequencing > year dumping.
      Knowing “1857” and “1885” as isolated facts isn’t enough;
      Prelims tests whether you can place them relative to each other.

    • Classic trap area.
      Chronology questions are high-frequency and high-error —
      Timeline Sorter trains exactly that pattern.

    • Explanation after every set
      so a miss becomes learning, not a guessed streak.

1v1 Community Battles — practice like it’s personal

What you do:
    Challenge another warrior from the community.
    Pick a subject (or mix). Face 10 timed MCQs. See who scores higher.

Why it helps Prelims:

    • Solo revision is easy to postpone.
      A live challenge creates a deadline — you show up and attempt.

    • Timed questions mimic exam pressure:
      decide fast, don’t overthink every option into a blank.

    • Competing on the same set exposes weak topics quickly —
      if you lose on Polity or Modern History, you know what to revise next.

    • Accountability from peers beats another unread PDF.
      One battle tonight > “I’ll do a mock tomorrow.”
