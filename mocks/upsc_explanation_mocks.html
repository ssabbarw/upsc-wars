State ratification threshold under Article 368
Question
Regarding state ratification under the constitutional amendment procedure, which one of the following is correct?
aRatification must be completed within six months by the required states
bAll state legislatures must ratify the amendment for it to be validYour pick
cRatification by not less than one-half of the states is required, no time limit prescribedCorrect
dAll state assemblies must ratify, though legislative councils may be bypassed
Explanation
Article 368 establishes a three-tier amendment procedure. The most stringent tier requires a special majority in Parliament AND ratification by not less than one-half of the states — a numerical majority, not unanimity. No time limit is prescribed.
Value addition
· Ratification must come from state legislatures, not governments or governors.
· The President cannot withhold assent — made mandatory by the 24th Amendment.
· Only 8–10 categories of provisions require state ratification.
Deep dive
Watch out — option (d)
Option (d) uses "all state assemblies" — sounds authoritative but unanimity is never the standard under Article 368.
1Eliminate (a): no time limit prescribed — the Constitution is silent on this.
2Eliminate (b): "all states" is never required — only a majority of state legislatures.
3Eliminate (d): "all states" is an overstatement — threshold is more than half.
4Confirm (c): not less than one-half of states, no time limit — exactly what Article 368 specifies.
Students conflate the state ratification requirement with unanimity, reasoning by analogy with the US federal system. India requires only a majority of states. The no-time-limit detail is a secondary trap for candidates who assume Parliament can set a deadline.

Panchama category and Shudra internal differentiation
Question
Consider the following statements regarding the emergence of the 'Panchama' category and internal differentiation within the Shudra Varna:
1. The term 'Panchama' was an established feature of the four-fold Varna system from the Rigvedic period itself.
2. Untouchable communities are placed outside the four-Varna system, not merely at the bottom of the Shudra category.
3. The sat-Shudra / asat-Shudra distinction reflects internal differentiation within the Shudra Varna based on occupational associations.
a1 and 2 onlyYour pick
b2 only
c2 and 3 onlyCorrect
d1, 2 and 3
Explanation
The 'Panchama' label is a post-Vedic construct — projecting it onto the Rigvedic period is an anachronism. Untouchable communities sit entirely outside the four-Varna hierarchy, not at the bottom of the Shudra category. The sat/asat-Shudra division distinguishes those in clean service occupations from those in ritually polluting ones.
Value addition
· Manusmriti categorises Chandalas as progeny of the most degraded Pratiloma union — textually outside the Varna system.
· R.S. Sharma's materialist thesis links asat-Shudra exclusion to bonded agricultural labour.
· The Arthashastra references Shvapakas, predating Manusmriti's codification.
Deep dive
1 The term 'Panchama' was an established feature of the Varna system from the Rigvedic period itself. Incorrect
Panchama is a later construct. The Rigvedic Purusha Sukta presents only a four-fold Varna — projecting Panchama onto it is a textbook anachronism.
2 Untouchable communities are placed outside the four-Varna system, not merely at the bottom of the Shudra category. Correct
This is the critical distinction — untouchables form a category beyond the Varna framework entirely, reflected in Manusmriti's treatment of Chandalas.
3 The sat-Shudra / asat-Shudra distinction reflects internal differentiation based largely on occupational associations. Correct
Sat-Shudras (potters, barbers) served upper castes and remained touchable; asat-Shudras in polluting occupations were progressively excluded from the Varna system altogether.
Watch out — option (a)
Students who correctly recall Statement 2 but also wrongly accept Statement 1 land on option (a). The Panchama anachronism is the trap that inflates option (a)'s appeal.
1Statement 1 incorrect — Panchama is post-Vedic; eliminate options that include it as correct (a, d).
2Statement 2 correct — untouchables outside Varna entirely; retain options containing 2.
3Statement 3 correct — sat/asat-Shudra distinction is occupational; retain options containing 3.
4Only option (c) has 2 and 3 correct, 1 excluded — confirm (c).
The temporal-swap trap projects a later category (Panchama) onto the Rigvedic period. Students who know the Purusha Sukta divides society into four parts may assume awareness of a 'fifth' — but the text presents only four Varnas. The trap works because Panchama is a real term, just from a much later period of Dharmashastra literature.
